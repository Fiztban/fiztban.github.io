This guide is designed to cover everything you need to know about the assessment and what it includes.

This is one pathway covering both ADHD and autism, for adults aged 18 and over. A diagnosis of either requires observations that identify it in both childhood and adulthood, so the assessment gathers evidence from both.

What is required for the assessment

  1. School reports. School reports are a very important source of evidence about your childhood. Please check whether you have any school reports, from any year at school, that include your teachers’ comments on your aptitude at school. One report is enough, but more are welcome.
  2. An observer for the DIVA and the ADI-R. The DIVA (for ADHD) and the ADI-R (for autism) are structured interviews with someone who knows you well, to take the history the assessment needs. Ideally this is the same person for both, and someone who has known you well from childhood through to today. The ADI-R focuses on your early years, so someone who knew you as a young child is best placed. The best candidates are parents, followed by siblings, close relatives, partners and friends.

If you have no school reports, your observer needs to be a parent, sibling or close relative who has known you well from childhood to today, as they will then be our main source of evidence about your school years. If neither is possible, please talk to us before going any further, as we may not be able to complete the assessment.

You can check you meet both requirements in step 2.

Appointment options

The two observer interviews — for ADHD (DIVA) and for autism (ADI-R) — are held online. The ADOS-2 observation must be carried out in person at our Whangārei clinic. All other appointments can be held either online via telehealth or in person.

Assessment components and pathway options

Neither assessment can rest on a single appointment. Both sets of diagnostic criteria ask for evidence from childhood and from adulthood, across more than one setting. A combined assessment therefore runs all four components — the two belonging to the ADHD assessment and the two belonging to the autism assessment. All four have to happen.

If any assessment component is not completed, we cannot finish the assessment.

All four components are brought to our multidisciplinary team meeting, led by Dr Castle, before the final appointment. Note that no component on its own produces a diagnosis.

When the component prices apply

The component prices above are charged only when the assessment does not run to completion — either because you choose to stop after a component is finished, or because what we have learned points somewhere else.

As your school reports come in, or during the interviews and the observation, the picture can change. If it does, we would discuss the options with you. Typically these are:

  • Neither ADHD nor autism seems likely, and we suggest discontinuing the assessment.
  • ADHD alone seems likely, and we suggest moving to our Adult ADHD Assessment.
  • Autism alone seems likely, and we suggest moving to our Autism (ASD) Assessment.

Where we move to another assessment, any component already completed that also forms part of that pathway is covered by its price — you are not charged twice. Only components that sit outside the new pathway are charged at the prices above, and we will always set out the figures with you first.

ADHD medication

ADHD medication is often a helpful part of managing ADHD, and we can offer to start and manage it following a positive diagnosis. Medication commencement and management is a separate service, started after completion of this assessment. Read below for more information.

  5. 1 hour Online only Nurse Specialist

    Once you have sent your school reports in step 4, we will assist in booking the ADHD observer interview. If you let us know you have reports on the way, we can book it in parallel.

    As you have no school reports to send, we will assist in booking the ADHD observer interview once step 3 is complete.

    This is a one-hour appointment with a set structure, using the Diagnostic Interview for ADHD in Adults (DIVA). The aim is to take a detailed history for the ADHD part of your assessment, covering how things were for you growing up and how they are for you now.

    Your observer is the person you thought of in step 2. Ideally they have known you well from childhood through to today, as the interview covers both. If possible, the same person also takes part in the autism observer interview in step 6, which focuses on your early years — so someone who knew you as a young child is the best choice for both.

    The DIVA only requires your observer to attend. It is led by one of our Nurse Specialists (Chris or Kezia), who will also be the one to deliver post-diagnostic support once the assessment is complete.

  6. 1 hour Online only Nurse Specialist

    The Autism Diagnostic Interview–Revised (ADI-R) is an interview with your observer, looking for signs of autism in your early years. It only requires your observer to attend, is led by our Nurse Specialist and is held online.

    This covers the autism part of the assessment, and is separate from the ADHD observer interview in step 5. It works best with the same person: the ADI-R takes a history of your early childhood, so someone who knew you then — usually a parent — will be able to answer most of it. If that is not possible, please talk to us about who else could take part.

  7. 1 hour In person, Whangārei only

    The Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS-2) is a structured observation carried out with you, taking approximately one hour. It is a mix of conversation and activities, and there is nothing to prepare.

    This is the one appointment that must be completed face to face at our Whangārei clinic — it cannot be done online.

    To further support you, we include with the assessment one hour of post-diagnostic support (a single 1-hour appointment, or two 30-minute appointments), which you can request to book directly with the Nurse Specialist who led your observer interviews and already knows your history.

If medication is recommended

At Kinder Minds we do not start medication during the assessment. We consider this an important decision, and we encourage you to first consider the recommended strategies and take your time deciding whether medication is right for you — talking it over with the people close to you, if that helps.

Both the diagnosis appointment (step 9) and the post-diagnostic support session (step 12) are ideal times to discuss any questions you may have about medication. If you choose to commence medication, or have already done so and need it reviewed, our ADHD Titration Clinic explains how a medication trial is carried out.

Support after the diagnosis

Your pathway includes a one-hour post-diagnostic support session with one of our Nurse Specialists, which you can book in the month following your assessment (step 12). It is a chance to talk through what the diagnosis means, ask questions, and plan the next steps together.

If further therapeutic support would be helpful after that, we can refer you on to our post-diagnostic clinic.
